# Source: "The Research Paper Recipe" slide deck (37 slides, 6 modules)

Reference artifact. The deck is the seed for the verify-and-enrich research pass on the 15 chapters. Deck-module → chapter mapping and the deck's specific factual claims are distilled below for the research agents.

## Module → chapter mapping
- **M1 Before you write** (slides 1–4): papers fail before writing (fluency ≠ validity); topic→question→hypothesis ladder; build the argument/schema first (intent / evidence / structure layers); a claim that can't fail is advocacy. → **ch01**, parts of **ch02**.
- **M2 Foundation & assertion types** (slides 5–9): assertion taxonomy (descriptive, causal, correlational, predictive, mechanistic, normative); falsifiability (Popper; sponge that absorbs everything proves nothing; Cartesian "what would I have to observe to conclude this is wrong?"); null vs alternative (H₀ is a baseline not "nothing"; fail-to-reject ≠ proof of null, could be underpowered); match design to claim (causal → RCT/strong quasi-exp; no statistical workaround). → **ch02**, **ch03**, **ch04**.
- **M3 Measurement** (slides 10–14): the hierarchy (hypothesis→design→data→interpretation, a flaw upstream corrupts downstream); measure–construct gap (Plato's cave; "the score is not the student"; validity/reliability/alignment); five measurement questions (aligned to intervention? baseline comparison? population represented? timescale retrieval-vs-storage? would the design detect a false result?); retrieval strength ≠ storage strength (Bjork; massed = high immediate/low durable, spaced = low immediate/high durable; learning claims need a delayed measure). → **ch05**.
- **M4 Data quality & statistics** (slides 15–24): GIGO (sophisticated analysis of bad data → confident-looking bad results; GIGO check happens before any test); a p-value is not the probability you're right (report test statistic, df, exact p, effect size); SE vs SD and large samples (SD = spread, doesn't shrink with n; SE = SD/√n, shrinks with n; t = Δ/SE so n↑→SE↓→t↑→p↓; 10,000-employee example: 0.4 pts on 100-scale → p=.0001 but d=0.04; Fisher built p-values in the 1920s for ~30-row field plots); degrees of freedom & t vs Z (df = n − constraints; fewer df → fatter tails → larger t needed; Z assumes known σ, t estimates σ from sample; df=60 → t 2.00 vs Z 1.96; df≈200 agree to 3 digits; df=∞ → t=Z; Gosset published t as "Student" at Guinness in 1908); effect size is the honest number (d=(M₁−M₂)/SD, √n never enters; Cohen 0.2/0.5/0.8 small/medium/large; ASA warned against over-reliance on p<.05 in 2016); what breaks at scale (df correction gone by n≈60, t-vs-Z by n≈120, p-as-filter by n≈10,000; effect size, CIs, practical significance stay honest); garden of forking paths (p-hacking often unconscious, HARKing, pre-registration separates confirmatory from exploratory); report everything but lead with effect size + CI; large-n p-value problem ("could this be noise?" ≠ "does this matter?"). → **ch06**, **ch07**, **ch08**.
- **M5 Drafting** (slides 25–32): research order comes before writing order (idea → preliminary search → **refine the question (where research is won or lost)** → deep lit review → methods design → data collection/analysis → results → discussion → introduction → abstract → title); reading/writing orders (naive: title→abstract→intro→methods→results→discussion; expert: title→abstract→**figures/tables**→discussion→methods→intro; writing: methods→results→discussion→**intro**→abstract→title); Methods = transparent enough to replicate (checklist); Results = report what happened, nothing else (no "suggests"/"demonstrates"); Discussion is the argument (4 moves: central finding+meaning; connect to prior work with a stance; address null/unexpected; limitations+implications; never open with results restatement or close with "more research is needed"); CARS funnel — territory→niche→occupation (Swales; weak intros fail at Move 2); abstract = 5 sentences (problem, gap, method, finding, implication; finding most often missing). → **ch09**, **ch10**.
- **M6 Quality control & submission** (slides 33–37): calibrate claims to evidence (scale: "raises the possibility"=single case → "suggests"=one correlational → "indicates"=correlational large N → "demonstrates"=RCT → "proves"=RCT multi-site replicated; audit every "causes/leads to/produces"); fog audit (nominalization, vague quantifier, throat-clearing, over-hedging — over-hedging is as dishonest as overclaiming); ethics screen (selective reporting→pre-register; overgeneralization→scope to sample; undisclosed AI use→disclose; confirmatory review→include disconfirming); the complete sequence (Thinking → Writing → Auditing with phase gates). → **ch11**, **ch12**, **ch13**, **ch14**.

## Deck's specific factual claims to verify against primary/current sources
1. Fisher developed significance testing / the p-value in the 1920s for small (~30-observation) agricultural field plots; clearing t≈2 was a real filter then.
2. Gosset published the t-distribution under the pen name "Student" in 1908, working on small samples at the Guinness brewery.
3. Cohen's d conventional benchmarks: 0.2 small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large.
4. The American Statistical Association formally warned against over-reliance on p<.05 in 2016.
5. t vs Z numeric convergence: df=60 → t≈2.00 vs Z=1.96; agree to ~3 sig figs by df≈200; equal at df=∞.
6. Bjork's storage-strength vs retrieval-strength distinction; spaced > massed for durable retention.
7. Swales' CARS model (Create A Research Space) — territory/niche/occupation moves.
8. Popper's falsifiability; asymmetry of confirmation vs refutation.
9. p-hacking (Simmons, Nelson & Simonsohn 2011), HARKing (Kerr 1998), garden of forking paths (Gelman & Loken 2013/2014), pre-registration / registered reports.
10. The 10,000- / 50,000-employee large-n examples (illustrative — keep labeled as such).
