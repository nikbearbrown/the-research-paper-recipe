## Preface

This book began as a talk for HCI Summer Crunch at Northeastern: a summer writing group built around a simple goal, to help participants finish a manuscript by the end of the summer and submit it to CHI, IEEE VR, TEI, IUI, or another venue that fit the work. The group met every other Monday from 10 to noon. The first hour brought everyone together around faculty guests, inspirational papers, and a specific writing problem, such as data visualization, the use of figures, or the structure of an argument. The second hour turned toward one-on-one feedback with peers.

That format shaped the book. HCI Summer Crunch welcomed undergraduate, master's, PhD, and faculty participants. It did not require anyone to arrive with a finished draft or completed data collection. What mattered was the work of turning a question, study, analysis, or design project into a manuscript that could be read, criticized, improved, and eventually submitted.

This book exists because that work needs a usable path. Researchers do not usually fail because they lack words. They fail because the claim is unclear, the evidence is mis-scoped, the graph hides the result, the literature review does not make the question feel inevitable, or the prose sounds more confident than the study permits. *The Research Paper Recipe* was written to gather those problems into a sequence: first the claim, then the evidence, then the measurement, analysis, figures, literature, draft, review, ethics, and prose.

The urgency is practical. Researchers now write with search engines, AI systems, dashboards, tutorials, partial answers, and overwhelming literatures open around them. That abundance helps, but it also creates a new problem: it is easy to collect explanations without building judgment. This book is meant to slow the writer down just enough to see the structure of the paper they are trying to make.

The authorial stance is applied and instructional. The goal is not to be encyclopedic. The goal is to give readers enough language, examples, and order that they can continue writing with better questions.
