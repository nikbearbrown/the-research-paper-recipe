# The Research Paper Recipe — Deck Outline

**Audience:** advanced undergrads / early grad students.
**Design system:** NEU (Northeastern). Lato, sentence-case, regular-weight headings, NU Red #C8102E accent, squared corners, no gradients/shadows on type.
**Canvas:** 1920×1080.

## Slide-color system (visual rhythm)
- **White** = content slides (default surface)
- **Black** = module divider slides (parallel, eyebrow "Module 0X")
- **NU Red** = "Pause & Reflect" engagement slides (parallel, eyebrow "Pause")
- Recurring brand device: 32×4px red rule under titles; small red N monogram + page no. footer on content slides.

## Title style
Mostly short declarative sentences (action titles) as given in the blueprint — they read as a story when scanned top to bottom.

## Sequence (38 frames)
- Cover — The Research Paper Recipe / A complete workflow from raw curiosity to submission
- DIV Module 1 — Before You Write
- 1 Most papers fail before anyone writes a word  (split: cracked polished paper vs rough-but-solid structure)
- 2 Topic → Question → Hypothesis: three different things  (3-rung ladder + example table)
- 3 Build the argument before you build the prose  (Intent → Evidence → Structure chain)
- 4 PAUSE: What kind of claim are you making?
- DIV Module 2 — Foundation & Assertion Types
- 5 Not all claims are the same kind of thing  (6-row assertion taxonomy table)
- 6 A hypothesis must be able to fail  (sponge vs falsifiable frame)
- 7 The null is not "nothing" — it's your baseline  (H0/H1 fork → test → two paths)
- 8 Match your design to your claim — no exceptions  (assertion→design→weaker-design table)
- 9 PAUSE: Apply the Cartesian test
- DIV Module 3 — Measurement
- 10 A flaw at any level corrupts everything downstream  (4-stage vertical pipeline)
- 11 The number is not the thing  (construct ↔ instrument gap)
- 12 Five questions to ask of any outcome measure  (numbered list w/ icons)
- 13 Retrieval strength ≠ storage strength  (massed vs spaced retention SVG line chart)
- 14 PAUSE: Run the five questions on a paper you're citing
- DIV Module 4 — Data Quality & Statistics
- 15 Sophisticated analysis of bad data…  (data pipeline w/ failure-point warnings)
- 16 A p-value is not the probability you're right  (annotated stats output)
- 17 The garden of forking paths…  (forking-path decision tree)
- 18 What a complete result actually looks like  (incomplete vs complete before/after)
- 19 PAUSE: Audit a results paragraph
- DIV Module 5 — Drafting
- 20 Write the sections in discovery order, not display order  (reading vs writing order)
- 21 The Methods section: transparent enough to replicate  (8-item checklist)
- 22 Results: report what happened. Nothing else.  (OK vs move-to-Discussion two-col)
- 23 The Discussion is the argument. Argue.  (4-stage Discussion flow)
- 24 Three moves: territory → niche → occupation  (CARS funnel)
- 25 Five sentences. The complete argument.  (5-box abstract sequence)
- 26 PAUSE: The Feynman test for your draft
- DIV Module 6 — Quality Control & Submission
- 27 Match your language to your evidence  (calibration scale)
- 28 Fog is the distance between what a sentence says and what it means  (4 before/after pairs)
- 29 A paper that can't survive replication is an ethical failure  (4-item ethics audit)
- 30 The complete sequence  (3-phase ribbon: Thinking / Writing / Auditing)
- 31 The question to ask before you submit  (closing reflection — red)
